## 3. Your code and your data

- **Your code stays yours.** The Simulator runs in your web browser. Your robot code is never uploaded to a server and is not saved; it is gone when you close or reload the page. Largo Robotics claims no rights in your code.
- **Check-in.** The first time you open the Simulator in a browser, it asks two questions: your team number (or "not on a team") and your role (student, mentor, coach, parent or other). Your answers are sent to a Google Form owned by Largo Robotics, to count users, the teams they come from and how often the Simulator is used: once when you answer, and once more on each later day you open a Simulator page (marked as a return visit, with the same team number and role – at most once a day). No names or emails are asked. Your answer is remembered in your browser so you aren't asked again.
- **Usage counting.** The pages count visits (with the visitor's country and region), the team numbers typed onto robot cards, code uploads, match runs and which features are used (for example the 3-D view, online play, the controller brand, the driving mode, the browser's language and the kind of device), using GoatCounter, without cookies. If you open the Simulator from a campaign link (one ending in something like `?utm_campaign=…`), that campaign's name is remembered in your browser for 60 days and counted with those events and your check-in, so Largo Robotics knows which posts and posters reach teams. Nothing personal is collected.
- **Top AUTO leaderboard.** When an AUTO is played to 0:00, the AUTO page sends the team numbers, the score, a short fingerprint of the code (not the code itself) and whether the run counts, to a Google Form owned by Largo Robotics. Scores may be shown publicly on the Simulator. Largo Robotics may remove any score at any time.
- **Online matches.** When you use **Play online**, the laptops in the match connect directly to each other (WebRTC). The free PeerJS service (0.peerjs.com) only helps them find each other using the room code. The laptops share each player's robot card (team number and name, button setup, robot speeds and timing), controller input and robot commands, and the host's field. Your TeleOp code is never sent – it runs only on your laptop. Like any direct connection, the laptops in a match can see each other's internet (IP) address, so only share room codes with people you know. Room chat messages (text only) go only to the laptops in the room. Nothing from an online match – including chat – is saved or recorded.
- **Feedback.** Anything you send through the Feedback form may be used by Largo Robotics to improve the Simulator, without payment or credit.
- **Translation.** The language menu uses your browser's built-in translator, which runs on your own computer.
